**It is wall-clock based.** `Dispose` gives the writer `max(floor, allowance x queued)` = about 10 s
for the 100 queued entries, then counts what is left as dropped. The test assumes the writer clears
100 entries - each a 5 ms sleep and a file write - in that time, i.e. under 100 ms an entry. In the
failing run it wrote 36 in 10 s, about 280 ms an entry: a starved writer thread, not a defect. A
not-reproduced close is not the right outcome; the assumption is real and was broken by load.

**What the test must keep proving** (aud-a49): the wait scales with the queue. It rests on two
inequalities the sleeps guarantee - the drain takes longer than the floor (else a fixed, floor-only
wait passes it) and longer than one entry's allowance (else a wait that ignores the queue length
passes it); at 5 ms an entry the drain is at least 500 ms against 100 ms for both - and on a third
that only timing gives: the scaled bound outlasting the drain. Load broke the third. Raising the
allowance alone would break the second (500 ms of sleeps against a 1 s allowance), so the sleep goes
up with it.

Chosen, the aud-a67 (7) shape - structure kept, the wall-clock bound moved far above any stall:
- **Allowance 1 s an entry** (was 100 ms): the scaled bound for 100 queued becomes about 100 s, 3.5
  times the slowest drain seen (about 28 s at 280 ms an entry). `Dispose` returns the moment the
  writer finishes, so a healthy run is no slower for it.
- **20 ms of sleep an entry** (was 5 ms): the drain is then at least 2 s - guaranteed, `Thread.Sleep`
  never returns early - so it stays longer than the floor (100 ms, unchanged) and longer than one
  entry's allowance (1 s). Both regressions the test exists for still fail it, whatever the load.
- Everything else unchanged: the held first entry, the 100 queued, the assertions.

A healthy run takes about 2-3 s instead of 0.5-1.5 s. Not a deterministic completion signal: the
property under test is how long `Dispose` is willing to wait, so some real wait is the subject.

## Known gaps

- **Still a wall-clock test, by nature.** What it proves is how long `Dispose` is willing to wait;
  only a drain slower than 1 s an entry on average - 3.5 times the slowest seen - fails it now.
- **About 2-6 s instead of 0.5-1.5 s** per run, for the guaranteed 2 s drain.
- **The neighbouring test `Dispose_CountsWhatItCouldNotWriteInItsBound_BeforeItReturns`** is not
  this finding and was left alone. Its main assertion runs the other way - it needs `Dispose` to
  give up, with the writer held and a 1 ms allowance, which load cannot undo - but it has two 10 s
  waits of the kind moved here (`entered.Wait` for the consumer to start, and `SpinUntil` for the
  held entry to be written after release) that a stall that long would fail. Not seen failing;
  candidate if it is.
- **aud-a64 (5)** may change `Dispose`'s bound; if it does, the 100 ms floor and the 1 s allowance in
  this test are its seams and keep their meaning only while the bound is `max(floor, allowance x
  queued)`.
